## 🧮 DAY 4: Why Syringe Units Do NOT Equal Milligrams

```text
⚠️ The #1 most common and dangerous mistake in peptide research is confusing syringe UNITS with peptide MILLIGRAMS.

Let's clear this up once and for all:

• Milligrams (mg) measure MASS. It represents the weight of the dry peptide powder inside the vial.
• Syringe Units measure VOLUME. On a standard U-100 syringe, 100 units = exactly 1.0mL of liquid.

Because they measure different properties, they are NOT the same. Your liquid dose concentration depends entirely on the volume of Bacteriostatic Water you add.

🔬 Look at the concentration math variance for a 10mg vial:
• Add 1.0mL of water: 10 syringe units = 1.0mg of peptide.
• Add 2.0mL of water: 10 syringe units = 0.5mg of peptide.
• Add 3.0mL of water: 10 syringe units = 0.33mg of peptide.
```
